"Alright, first things first. This workbook has 9 different sheets, and they all work together. Let me give you a quick overview:

The **Dashboard** is your command center - it shows you the most important numbers at a glance.

The **Instructions** sheet - which I highly recommend reading - has definitions, best practices, and tips for presenting to allocation boards.

**Annual Budget** is where you'll plan your entire year's income and expenses.

**Semester Breakdown** helps you split your budget between Fall and Spring.

**Event Calculator** is perfect for planning individual events.

**Monthly Tracker** is where you'll track your actual spending throughout the year.

**Allocation Request** is a professional template for requesting student government funding.

**Year-End Report** helps you summarize your financial performance at the end of the year.

**White cells** are where YOU enter your data.
**Gray cells** contain formulas that auto-calculate - don't edit these!
**Red highlighting** means you're over budget - warning sign!
**Green highlighting** means you're under budget or doing well.

Got it? White = you fill in. Gray = don't touch. Red = problem. Green = good."

### SCRIPT

"Let's start building your budget! Click on the **Annual Budget** tab.

**Step 1: Organization Info**
At the top, enter your organization name and confirm the academic year. Simple enough!

**Step 2: Project Your Income**
Now we're going to plan where your money comes from. This is the INCOME section.

You might get money from:
- Student government allocations
- Fundraising
- Membership dues
- Event tickets
- Sponsorships
- And several other sources

Here's a pro tip: Be conservative with income projections. It's better to underestimate than to count on money you might not get. If you're not sure about a fundraiser, don't count that income yet.

Let me show you an example. Let's say your org expects:
- $8,000 from student government
- $1,500 from membership dues - that's 50 members at $30 each
- Maybe $2,000 from a fundraiser
- And you have $1,200 rolling over from last year

Watch what happens when I enter these numbers... [Type numbers]

See this gray cell at the bottom? It automatically adds up your Total Projected Income. In this case, $12,700. You didn't have to calculate that - it's done for you.

**Step 3: Plan Your Expenses**
Now let's plan where that money will go. Scroll down to the EXPENSE section.

Common expense categories for student orgs:
- Events & Programming - this is usually your biggest expense
- Food & Catering - students love free food!
- Marketing & Printing - flyers, social media ads
- Travel for conferences
- Supplies
- Speaker fees
- Equipment
- And more

Let me fill in some realistic amounts for our example org... [Type numbers while talking]

Events & Programming: $6,000
Food & Catering: $3,000
Marketing: $800
Travel: $1,500
Supplies: $500
Speaker fees: $1,000

Notice this line that says "Contingency Fund (10%)" - this automatically calculates 10% of your total expenses and sets it aside for emergencies. Things ALWAYS cost more than you expect, so this buffer is crucial. You don't enter anything here - it's automatic.

**Step 4: Check Your Budget Status**
Now scroll down to the BUDGET SUMMARY section.

This is the moment of truth. Your Net Position tells you if your budget is balanced.

See how it shows Total Income minus Total Expenses? If this number is:
- **Positive** (green) = You have a surplus, budget is balanced ✓
- **Zero** = Perfectly balanced
- **Negative** (red) = You have a deficit - you're planning to spend more than you have ⚠

In our example, we have a small surplus, which is great! The status says "BALANCED" with a checkmark.

If yours shows a deficit, you need to either increase your income projections or decrease your expenses. You can't spend money you don't have!"

## PART 4: ADDITIONAL PLANNING TOOLS (7:00 - 11:00)

### VISUAL
- Navigate to Semester Breakdown
- Show Event Calculator
- Briefly show Allocation Request

### SCRIPT

"**Semester Breakdown**

Your annual budget is done, but let's be real - you don't spend evenly throughout the year. Most orgs spend 60-70% of their budget in the Fall semester when everyone's excited and engaged.

Click on the **Semester Breakdown** tab.

This sheet helps you plan WHEN you'll spend your money. The Annual Total column pulls from your Annual Budget - you don't enter anything there.

Now you decide: How much of each category will you spend in Fall vs. Spring?

For example, if you budgeted $6,000 for events:
- Maybe $4,000 in Fall (you have a big Fall festival)
- And $2,000 in Spring (smaller events)

Enter those amounts in the Fall and Spring columns.

See this Variance column? If it shows $0, you're good - your Fall plus Spring equals your Annual Total. If it shows any other number, you need to adjust. This is your double-check to make sure you've allocated everything.

**Event Budget Calculator**

Now let's look at the **Event Calculator** tab. This is super useful for planning individual events.

Let's say you're planning a big dinner event. At the top, enter:
- Event name: "Annual Banquet"
- Date: April 15th
- Expected attendance: 100 students

Now list your income sources:
- Ticket sales: Let's say $10/ticket × 100 people = $1,000
- Maybe you got a $500 sponsorship

And your expenses:
- Venue: $300
- Food: $1,200 (that's $12 per person)
- Entertainment: $400
- Decorations: $150
- Marketing: $100

Watch these calculated fields at the bottom:
- **Cost per attendee**: Automatically calculates total expenses ÷ attendance
- **Break-even ticket price**: Shows what you need to charge per ticket to not lose money

In this example, our cost per attendee is $20.50. So if we only charge $10 per ticket, we're taking a $10.50 loss per person. That's okay if your org is subsidizing the event, but you need to know that number!

This helps you make smart decisions about ticket pricing.

**Allocation Request**

Quickly, let me show you the **Allocation Request** tab.

This is your professional template for requesting money from student government. When it's time for allocation hearings, you'll fill this out with:
- Your org information
- Detailed breakdown of what you're requesting
- Justification for each line item - this is crucial!
- Previous year performance
- Impact statement

The allocation board wants to know: Will you actually spend this money? How many students will benefit? Why should they fund you?

Fill this out thoughtfully, and you'll look incredibly prepared and professional."

## PART 5: TRACKING & REPORTING (11:00 - 15:00)

### VISUAL
- Navigate to Monthly Tracker
- Show Dashboard
- Show Year-End Report

### SCRIPT

"**Monthly Tracker**

Okay, your budget is planned. Now you need to actually TRACK your spending throughout the year. This is where most orgs fail - they make a great budget and then never update it.

Click on the **Monthly Tracker** tab.

This sheet covers 10 months - August through May for a typical academic year.

Here's how you use this:

At the START of each month, enter your budgeted amounts for that month. How much do you expect to bring in? How much do you plan to spend?

DURING or at the END of the month, enter your ACTUAL amounts. How much did you actually receive? How much did you actually spend?

The Variance column automatically calculates the difference:
- If you see RED, you spent more than budgeted - that's a warning sign
- If you see GREEN, you spent less than budgeted - you're doing well
- WHITE means you're right on target

Let me show you an example. In September:
- Budgeted income: $1,000
- Actual income: $1,200 (we got more than expected - nice!)
- Budgeted expenses: $800
- Actual expenses: $950 (spent a bit more than planned)

The Net Position column shows you're still positive, and the variance shows you're slightly over budget on expenses.

Scroll down to see Year-to-Date Totals - these automatically sum up all your months.

And check out the Running Balance section at the bottom. This is really important! It shows:
- Your starting balance (rollover from last year)
- Plus YTD income
- Minus YTD expenses
- Equals your Current Balance

If this Current Balance number gets close to zero or goes negative, you have a serious problem. You're about to run out of money!

**Pro tip:** Update this sheet at least once a month. Set a reminder on your phone. Weekly updates are even better.

**Dashboard**

Now let's look at your **Dashboard** - click on that tab.

This is what you should check weekly. It's your financial health snapshot.

At the top, you see your key metrics in big, bold numbers:
- Total Budget
- Year-to-Date Income
- Year-to-Date Expenses

Below that:
- Budget Health Status - Balanced or Deficit
- Available Funds - how much money you have right now
- Budget Utilization - what percentage of your budget you've spent

These all auto-update from your other sheets. You don't enter anything here - it's a dashboard, not an input sheet.

Use this for:
- Quick checks before making purchase decisions
- Updates at exec board meetings
- Personal peace of mind

If everything's green and your status says "BALANCED," you're doing great!

**Year-End Report**

Finally, let's look at the **Year-End Report** tab.

You'll use this at the end of the year to summarize your financial performance.

It compares BUDGETED vs. ACTUAL for:
- Every income source
- Every expense category
- Overall net position

The variance columns show:
- Dollar differences
- Percentage differences

Color coding helps you quickly see:
- RED = Areas where you overspent
- GREEN = Areas where you came in under budget

Fill in the Key Achievements section:
- How many events did you hold?
- How many students attended?
- How many new members?
- How much did you fundraise?

And calculate your Rollover funds - money that carries over to next year.

This report is perfect for:
- Presenting to your advisor
- Showing to allocation boards
- Training your successor
- Understanding what to improve next year

Variance analysis is a learning opportunity. If you budgeted $2,000 for travel but only spent $500, maybe you were too conservative. Or if you budgeted $500 for marketing but spent $1,200, you need to budget more next year.

Learn from these variances to make better budgets in the future."

### VISUAL

### SCRIPT

"Before we wrap up, let me give you some crucial tips that will make you a budget superstar.

**Budget Planning Tips:**

1. **Be conservative with income** - Underestimate what you'll earn, overestimate what you'll spend. Better to have extra money than to run short.

2. **Always include contingency** - That 10% buffer is not optional. Things will cost more than you expect. Food delivery fees, price increases, unexpected needs - they happen.

3. **Get real quotes** - Don't guess what things cost. Call the caterer, check the venue pricing, get actual quotes. Your budget should be based on reality, not hopes.

4. **Update monthly** - A budget you don't maintain is useless. Set a monthly reminder to update your tracker. I promise, your future self will thank you.

5. **Communicate with your team** - Your exec board needs to know the financial situation. Don't wait until there's a crisis.

6. **Keep receipts** - Document everything. You need proof of spending for audits and records.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid:**

❌ Don't commit to expenses before checking your budget
❌ Don't forget about tax and fees when budgeting
❌ Don't overestimate fundraising success
❌ Don't skip the contingency fund
❌ Don't wait until the last minute to track expenses

**When Presenting to Allocation Boards:**

✓ Know every line item cold
✓ Bring quotes and documentation
✓ Show how funding benefits students
✓ Be honest about past performance
✓ Dress professionally
✓ Practice your presentation

**Using the Example Sheet**

If you ever get stuck, click on the **Example** tab. This shows a completed budget for a fictional Computer Science Club. You can see:
- Realistic amounts
- How categories are allocated
- What a balanced budget looks like
- Proper formatting

Don't just copy it - every org is different - but use it as a reference.

**Remember:**

This template gives you the TOOLS, but your judgment and diligence make the difference.

Being a treasurer is a big responsibility, but it's also an incredible learning opportunity. You're gaining real-world finance skills that will serve you in your career, personal finances, and future leadership roles.

Start with the Annual Budget, update your Monthly Tracker regularly, and check your Dashboard often. Do those three things, and you'll be miles ahead of most student org treasurers.

**You've got this!**

If you need to watch any section again, use the timestamps in the video description. And read through the README documentation - it has even more detailed instructions and troubleshooting tips.

Good luck with your organization's finances! Now get out there and make a killer budget.

[End screen with key takeaways]"

## PRODUCTION NOTES

### Before Recording

**Preparation:**
1. Create a fresh copy of the template
2. Have example numbers ready to type
3. Test screen recording software
4. Ensure good audio quality
5. Close unnecessary browser tabs/notifications

**Screen Setup:**
- Set Excel to 125% or 150% zoom for visibility
- Use screen recording with cursor highlighting
- Record at 1080p minimum
- Consider using a presenter tool for emphasis

### During Recording

**Voice:**
- Speak clearly and at moderate pace
- Pause between major steps
- Use enthusiastic but professional tone
- Define any Excel/finance terms you use

**Visual:**
- Move cursor deliberately to what you're discussing
- Zoom in on important cells/formulas
- Use highlighter tool for emphasis
- Don't move too fast - viewers need time to process

**Pacing:**
- Take your time with Part 3 (Annual Budget) - most important
- You can move faster through later sections
- Pause after entering data to show calculations

### After Recording

**Editing:**
1. Add chapter markers for each part
2. Add text callouts for key points
3. Include timestamps in description
4. Add "slow down" sections where needed
5. Include background music (soft, non-distracting)
6. Add intro/outro graphics

**Video Description:**
Include:
- Timestamps for each section
- Link to download template
- Link to README documentation
- Key tips recap
- Contact information

**Publish:**
- Upload to YouTube (unlisted or public)
- Share with student org community
- Send to student government office
- Include in treasurer training materials
